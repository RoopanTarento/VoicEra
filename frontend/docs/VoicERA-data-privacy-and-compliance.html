VoicERA for government · Data privacy and compliance

Can we say it is compliant? Not yet. Here is why, and the way there.

1. We cannot honestly say "compliant" today. The package document leaves three answers blank: where call data is stored, whether it trains models, how long it is kept.
2. The department stays accountable. Under the DPDP Act it is the Data Fiduciary. VoicERA and Bhashini act on its behalf. Compliance is shown through contract, configuration and evidence, not a label.
3. What we can say is narrow, and true. The department approves every word the agent speaks. No department system is connected. See the say / do not say table on page 4.

The data lifecycle

Eight stages. Most are open.

Every stage in the life of a citizen's data needs an owner and an answer. Today only collection, notice and sharing have a clear owner. The rest wait on written answers from VoicEra and Bhashini.

Retention: what to settle

  • Set a schedule per data type: audio, transcript, survey dataset, logs. Do not accept "kept as long as needed".
  • Default to keeping less. If you do not need recordings, choose transcript only, or neither.
  • Logs have their own clock. Security logs may have to be kept for a fixed period under other rules (page 3).
  • Test deletion once, with a real record, before launch.

Transparency: what to publish

  • A plain privacy notice on the department site and read at the start of every call.
  • A register of processing: what data, why, who sees it, how long.
  • The list of processors and sub-processors, with where each is.
  • A named grievance contact and how fast it replies.

So how do we say it is compliant?

Say less now. Earn the rest with evidence.

"Compliant" is a result, not a feature. You get there by collecting proof for each obligation, then describing only what the proof supports.

Say, because it is true nowSay only with evidenceDo not say
The department approves the script and knowledge files before setup."Call data is stored in India." Needs a hosting letter."VoicERA is DPDP compliant."
No department system is connected in Platinum, so no direct access to your databases."Call data is not used to train models." Needs a written commitment."Your data is secure." Without an assessment report.
It is hosted on Bhashini and billed to Bhashini."Recordings are deleted after N days." Needs a tested procedure."It is a Digital Public Good." Until recognised.
VoicERA does not commit model output quality."We notify you of a breach within X hours." Needs a runbook."Citizens' consent is handled." It is the department's job.

Evidence to collect before you claim anything

  • A processing agreement with Bhashini and VoicEra that names purpose, security, deletion and breach duties
  • A data-flow diagram and the full list of sub-processors, with locations
  • A hosting letter on residency and approved infrastructure
  • Security description: encryption, access, activity logs, plus an independent test report
  • A retention schedule and a deletion test with a real record
  • A breach runbook and who calls whom
  • A privacy impact assessment for scheme and survey calls
  • The notice wording, reviewed by counsel, and a recording showing it is played

Ask VoicEra and Bhashini in writing

  • Where are call audio, transcripts and answers stored, and who can access them?
  • Which providers see audio or text, and where are they?
  • Is any call data used to train or improve a model?
  • How long is each data type kept, and can we set it?
  • How is a citizen's data deleted on request, and how fast?
  • What is your breach process and notice time?
  • What security testing has been done, by whom?
  • What is the licence, and is DPG recognition planned?

A sample notice, 10 seconds. Needs legal review.

"This is an automated assistant for [Department]. We use what you tell us to [purpose]. This call may be recorded and kept for [period]. You can ask for a person, or ask us to delete your data, at [contact]."

What to confirm exists before this claim is easy

  • Retention and deletion controls per data type, confirmed in writing
  • Consent and notice records kept with each call
  • Fine-grained roles and an audit log of who saw what
  • Outbound controls: calling hours, opt-out and do-not-disturb handling
  • Authenticated, encrypted media connections, confirmed in writing
  • A processing-agreement template and a published processor list

None of these is described in the supplied package document. Ask whether each exists in the managed Platinum service.
